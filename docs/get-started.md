# Getting Started: Your first day

## What we're building

Imagine it's your first day at a new job. New desk, new laptop, new people, and about forty names you'll have forgotten by lunch. It also happens to be your first day with Vellum. So let's spend the day together and see what it can do.

Of course, there's one more thing on our minds. Coffee. Somebody here must know where the good coffee is. Right? We'll write that question down first thing this morning, and we'll see how long it takes to get an answer.

By five o'clock we'll have a handful of pages like these, and most of what's on them will have put itself there.

So let's see what we'll be building:

![Today's page at five o'clock: the day's meetings, with their notes and questions tucked under them](/images/get-started/open-today.webp)

Today's page, at the end of the day. Four meetings and a write-up, the notes we took in each, and the questions that came up along the way. We typed all of it as we went, one line at a time.

![Priya's page: her details, the questions waiting for her, and everywhere she was mentioned](/images/get-started/open-priya.webp)

This is Priya, who's showing us the ropes. Her team and her role at the top, the questions we're saving for her, and every place we mentioned her during the day. We never went to her page to write any of that down. (And don't worry, we'll explain what "her page" means in just a moment.)

![Everyone in a table, grouped by team](/images/get-started/open-people.webp)

Everyone we met today, in one table, grouped by team (along with a few people the vault came with).

![The Onboarding project, with its tasks gathered underneath](/images/get-started/open-project.webp)

Our onboarding, as a project, with the tasks that belong to it gathered underneath.

![The questions still open, grouped by who to ask](/images/get-started/open-questions.webp)

Every question we haven't had answered yet, wherever we wrote it, grouped by who to ask. Tick one off and it leaves the list.

![The write-up for Dana, a short document with links in it](/images/get-started/open-writeup.webp)

And at five o'clock our manager wants a few lines on how the day went. That becomes a proper piece of writing, with links to the people in it.

Vellum gives us three ways to capture information, and we'll use all three today. **Bullets**, one short line per thought, tucked under each other, which is how most of the day gets written. **Documents**, plain flowing writing for when a thought needs paragraphs. And **tags**, which say what kind of thing a note is, and can carry details like someone's team or phone number. There's no separate kind of "page" to make first. Any note can be opened as a page of its own.

That's where we're headed. So let's get started.

NB: `Cmd` throughout is the Mac key. Use `Ctrl` on Windows and Linux.

## Setting up as we go

We won't set anything up in advance. Everything gets made the moment the day needs it, on a note we actually want.

### A first look around

Open Vellum, and the first thing we see is today's page.

![Today's page in a brand-new vault: the Today's tasks box, then three lines of welcome](/images/get-started/first-open.webp)

The heading is today's date. Every day gets a page of its own, and they all live together under **Daily Notes**, the small word just above the date. The two little arrows beside the date step to the day page before this one and the day page after it. In a brand-new vault there aren't any other days yet, so both arrows are greyed out and do nothing. They "wake up" as soon as there's another day to step to.

Under the date there's a box called **TODAY'S TASKS**. We'll come back to it in a minute.

And under that, three lines Vellum wrote for us. Each of those lines is a **note**. The dot in front of a note is its **bullet**. A note can hold other notes tucked underneath it, and two of these do: see the faint ring around the bullets of **Learn a bit about Vellum** and **Thank you for being here**? That ring means there's more folded away inside.

### The rail and the sidebar

Two panels fold away at the sides of the window, and we'll use both all day. So let's get the names straight now, because you'll hear them a lot, in this guide and in our Vellum Discord server.

Click the icon at the very top left of the window (or press `Cmd-\`). A panel slides out on the left. That's the **navigation rail**. It's how we get around: **Today**, **Daily Notes** and the **Library** at the top, then **Tasks** and **Projects**, and further down anything pinned. You'll also hear people call it the left panel, or the rail. Same thing.

Now hold `Shift` and click the bullet in front of **Learn a bit about Vellum**. It opens on the right, under the word **SIDEBAR**. That's the **sidebar**, a place to keep a note open beside whatever page we're on. The icon near the top right of the window opens and closes it (so does `Cmd-Shift-\`).

![The whole window: the rail on the left, today's page in the middle, and the welcome tour open in the sidebar on the right](/images/get-started/rail-and-sidebar.webp)

Rail on the left, sidebar on the right. That's all the naming we need.

Have a read of the tour in the sidebar if you like. Then click the two icons again to put both panels away. We'll bring them back when we need them.

### Your first task

Back to that box at the top of the page, **TODAY'S TASKS**. This is where everything we've planned for today turns up, wherever we happened to write it down. A new vault puts one task in it for us: **Check off your first task**.

So ... let's do exactly that. Click the little square in front of it.

![The Today's tasks box after ticking: Nothing scheduled for today, and Done today now reads 2](/images/get-started/first-task-done.webp)

The task leaves the list, the box says **Nothing scheduled for today**, and the count beside **DONE TODAY** goes up by one. (If it already said 1 before you ticked anything, that's one of the sample tasks a new vault comes with, already done.)

Keep an eye on this box. Every morning starts here, and before lunch we'll give it a real task of our own.

### Making room

Those three lines were Vellum saying hello. Nice of it, but this is OUR day, and we'd like the page to ourselves. So let's clear them away. It's also a good excuse to learn how to select things, which we'll be doing all the time.

**A box around them.** Put the mouse in the empty space just to the left of the first bullet, a little above it. Press, and drag down and to the right, across the three lines. A faint box follows the mouse, and every line it touches lights up.

![The three welcome lines selected, each one highlighted](/images/get-started/select-box.webp)

Now press `Delete` (or `Backspace`, whichever your keyboard has). Vellum checks with us first.

![The Delete? box, listing what is about to go, with Keep and Delete buttons](/images/get-started/delete-dialog.webp)

It tells us exactly what's about to go: the three lines, and the seven notes folded away inside them. That's the welcome tour, and the first task we just ticked. Click **Delete**.

The lines are gone, and in their place the page says **Click to start typing**. The count beside **DONE TODAY** drops back by one as well, because the task we ticked lived inside **Learn a bit about Vellum** and went with it. The one still there is that sample task from earlier, which lives in the Library. NB: we'll talk about the Library a bit later.

**Changed your mind?** Press `Cmd-Z`. Everything comes back, exactly as it was. `Cmd-Z` takes back the last change to our notes, and pressing it again keeps going further back, so it's worth getting used to early. (`Cmd-Shift-Z` puts a change back again.)

**The keyboard way.** Now let's clear them for good, this time from the keyboard. Click at the end of **Welcome to Vellum!** so the cursor sits there, and press `Cmd-A`. The words on that line are selected, the same as in any text box. Press `Cmd-A` again, and the whole line lights up, as a line this time rather than as words.

![Welcome to Vellum! selected as a whole line, the other two lines not](/images/get-started/select-one-line.webp)

Press `Cmd-A` a third time and the selection grows to every line on the page. Each press reaches a little further out.

There's one more way, for when the mouse is already in hand. Press inside the words of one line and drag down into the next. As soon as the mouse crosses into another line, Vellum stops selecting words and selects whole lines instead.

However we got there, press `Delete`, click **Delete** in the box, and today's page is all ours.

![Today's page, cleared: the tasks box, and under it only Click to start typing](/images/get-started/day-empty.webp)

A clean page. Time to go to our first meeting.

## 9:00, the welcome meeting

Click where it says **Click to start typing**, type `9:00 Welcome meeting`, and press `Enter`.

`Enter` makes a new line below, a brand-new note. But the notes we're about to take are ABOUT the meeting, so let's tuck them underneath it. Press `Tab`, and the new line steps in, under the meeting. Now type these, pressing `Enter` after each one:

- `Team standup is every morning at 9:30`
- `First week is mostly reading and meeting people`
- `Laptop setup with IT this afternoon`

Then the one that really matters. Type `Where's the good coffee?` and press `Cmd-Enter`. A small square appears in front of it, a **checkbox**. A checkbox turns a line into something we can tick off, and this question gets ticked the moment somebody answers it.

Press `Enter` for the next line. Notice the new line comes with a checkbox of its own. Vellum guesses we're writing a list of things to tick. Not this time. `Cmd-Enter` goes round in a circle: the first press puts a box on, the second ticks it, the third takes it off. So press it twice, and the box goes.

Now press `Tab` again, and the line steps in a second level, under the coffee question. Type `Nobody knows. Dana only drinks tea`. (Tea?! We'll try not to hold it against her.)

Press `Enter`, then `Shift-Tab`. The line steps back out a level, in line with the other notes. Type `Dana wants a few lines at five on how the day went`. Then click an empty part of the page to stop typing.

![The welcome meeting with its notes tucked under it, the coffee question with a checkbox, and the tea line tucked under that](/images/get-started/meeting-notes.webp)

That's an outline: notes tucked under notes, as deep as we like. `Tab` tucks a line in, `Shift-Tab` brings it back out, and the thin line down the left shows what belongs to what.

**A time at the start.** Look just above the meeting. A small **9:00** has appeared. Starting a line with a time makes it one of the day's moments, and Vellum lists them above the day's notes. Hover over the time to see which line it belongs to, and click it to jump straight there. With one meeting that's not much to look at. By five o'clock it'll read like a timetable of our day.

![Hovering the 9:00 above the notes shows Welcome meeting](/images/get-started/moment-hover.webp)

**Folding away.** When a note has others tucked under it, we can fold them out of sight. Move the mouse over **9:00 Welcome meeting**, and a small arrow shows up to the left of its bullet. Click it. The notes fold away, and the bullet gets the same faint ring we saw on the welcome lines, the one that says there's more inside. Click the arrow again to open it back up. From the keyboard, `Cmd-Up` folds the line the cursor is in, and `Cmd-Down` opens it.

![The welcome meeting folded, its arrow showing and a ring around its bullet](/images/get-started/meeting-folded.webp)

**Moving things around.** Let's be honest, the coffee question is the most important thing on this page. So let's move it to the top. Press on its bullet, the dot in front of it, and drag it up above **Team standup is every morning at 9:30**. A line shows where it'll land. Let go.

![The coffee question now first under the meeting, with the tea line still tucked under it](/images/get-started/coffee-first.webp)

The tea line came along with it. Whatever is tucked under a note moves with the note. From the keyboard, `Cmd-Shift-Up` and `Cmd-Shift-Down` move the line the cursor is in.

Priorities sorted.

## Dana, our first tag

Dana deserves more than a passing mention. She's somebody we'll be dealing with every day, so let's give her a note of her own.

Click at the end of **Dana wants a few lines at five on how the day went** and press `Enter`. Type `Dana Whitfield #pers`. The moment we type the `#`, a list opens.

![Typing Dana Whitfield #pers, with #person offered at the top of the list and Create #pers under it](/images/get-started/tag-picker.webp)

That `#` is how we **tag** a note. A tag says what kind of thing a note is: a person, a project, a book, whatever we like. A new vault already comes with a few tags, and **#person** is right there at the top, with its description, **A person worth tracking.** Press `Enter` to take it. (The line underneath would make a brand-new tag called #pers instead. We'll make a tag of our own soon enough.)

Dana's name now wears a small **#person** label. We call that a **pill**.

One more thing while we're on her line. Press `Shift-Enter`. Instead of a new note, we get a second, quieter line under her name, for a few words about her. Type `Sits by the window. Takes her tea very seriously`, then click an empty part of the page.

![Dana Whitfield at the end of the meeting's notes, wearing a #person pill, with her second line under her name](/images/get-started/dana-line.webp)

(We'll chat about the faint **Add fields** under her in just a moment.)

### Her page

Remember the dot in front of every note? Click the one in front of **Dana Whitfield**.

![Dana's page: her name, her second line, the #person pill, then Fields, Notes and Books](/images/get-started/dana-page.webp)

This is Dana's page. Any note can be opened like this, as a page of its own, and a note that wears a tag gets a proper layout: the tag's pill under the name (with **+ Tag** beside it to add another), a **FIELDS** box for details about her, a **NOTES** section for anything we write on her page, and a **BOOKS** section, which we'll deal with in a moment.

Look at the very top of the page as well. That line of small words is the **breadcrumb** (or just crumb): **Daily Notes ›** today's date **› 9:00 Welcome meeting**. It says where this note lives, inside the welcome meeting on today's page. Click any part of the breadcrumb to go there.

### A shelf we don't need

Your vault may have given every person a **BOOKS** section, for the books they wrote. That's handy for Marcus Aurelius. Less so for our manager. (If your Dana has no Books section, skip ahead to the next part.)

Sections like this can be moved, hidden or removed. Move the mouse to the right-hand end of the **BOOKS** heading line, and a few small controls appear.

![The Books heading with its controls showing at the right: up and down arrows, Hide, and a slider icon](/images/get-started/books-hover.webp)

- The **↑** and **↓** arrows move the section up or down the page.
- **Hide** tucks it away, on this page only.
- The slider icon at the very end opens the section's sort, group and filter options, the same ones we'll meet on tag pages later on.

Click **Hide**. Books disappears, and a **HIDDEN** line turns up at the bottom of the page with **Books** in it. Click that, and it comes straight back.

![Dana's page with Books hidden, and a Hidden line at the bottom holding Books](/images/get-started/books-hidden.webp)

But Hide only tidies this one page. Every other person would still get the shelf. To take it off everyone at once, we change the tag itself, because the tag decides what every person's page looks like. Click the **#person** pill.

![The #person tag page: Notes 5 and Schema tabs, and every person in the vault listed](/images/get-started/person-tag-page.webp)

This is the tag's own page. Under **Notes** it lists every note wearing the tag: Dana, and the four people a new vault comes with. (Marcus Aurelius and Viktor Frankl wear **#author**, which counts as a kind of person. That's what **2 from subtags** is telling us.)

Now click **Schema**, the tab beside Notes. Schema is where a tag's setup lives: its fields, its sections, and a few more things we'll meet later. Scroll down to **SECTIONS**.

![The Sections list on the person tag: Notes, and Books showing every #book whose Author points here](/images/get-started/person-sections.webp)

Two sections: **Notes**, and **Books**, which shows **every #book whose Author points here**. Move the mouse over the Books row and click the `×` at its end. Vellum asks first.

![Delete Books? The books keep their details, the pages just stop collecting them](/images/get-started/delete-books.webp)

The books themselves aren't touched. Meditations still says Marcus Aurelius wrote it. The person pages just stop collecting books. Click **Delete**.

Now click the back arrow at the top left of the window. It takes us back to Dana's page, and the shelf is gone. It's gone from every person's page, Marcus Aurelius included.

To get back to today, click the date in the crumb. Or, from anywhere in Vellum, press `Cmd-D`, which always takes us to today's page.

## Links that work both ways

Now that Dana has a note of her own, the lines that mention her can point straight at it.

Look at **Dana wants a few lines at five on how the day went**. Double-click the word **Dana** so it's selected, and type `[[` (two square brackets). The word is wrapped in brackets and a list opens, already searching for "Dana".

![Dana wrapped in brackets, with Dana Whitfield at the top of the list that opened](/images/get-started/link-picker.webp)

**Dana Whitfield** is at the top. Press `Enter`. The word becomes a **link**, and it now reads **Dana Whitfield**, in blue. A link always shows the name of the note it points at, as that note spells it. If Dana's name ever changes, every link to her changes with it.

Click the link. It opens Dana's page, and something new has appeared at the bottom.

![Dana's page with a References section at the bottom, showing the line that mentions her, and a small circled 1 beside her name](/images/get-started/dana-references.webp)

A **REFERENCES** section, and in it, **MENTIONED IN…**, the line we just wrote on today's page. We never came to Dana's page to put that there. Linking to a note is enough for that note to know about it.

A term you might hear is "bidirectional linking" ... meaning a link that works in two directions. That's what we just made. The link in "Dana Whitfield wants a few lines ..." points to Dana's page, AND the References section at the bottom of Dana's page points back to every note that mentions her.

And look beside her name at the top, on the right. A small circled **1**. That's her **reference count**, how many places link to her.

Press `Cmd-D` to go back to today, and let's link her once more. In **Nobody knows. Dana only drinks tea**, double-click **Dana**, type `[[`, and press `Enter` to take **Dana Whitfield** again.

![Today's page with both lines linking to Dana Whitfield, and a circled 2 at the end of Dana's own line](/images/get-started/dana-count.webp)

Now look at the end of Dana's own line, down at the bottom of the meeting. The count there says **2**. It counts as we go.

That circle is also a button. Click it.

![Dana's line with the circle clicked, and both lines that mention her opened right under it](/images/get-started/dana-drawer.webp)

Both mentions open right there, under her line, without leaving the page. Handy when we want to know where somebody came up today. Click the circle again to close it.

## A tag of our own

**#person** came with the vault. But nothing in a new vault fits "a question I need answered", and today is going to be full of those. So let's make a tag of our own.

Click at the end of **Where's the good coffee?** and type ` #question` (with a space before the hashtag). There's no #question tag yet, so this time the list offers just one thing, **Create #question**.

![Typing #question after the coffee question, with Create #question offered](/images/get-started/create-question.webp)

Press `Enter`. And ... that's it. No setup screen, no form to fill in. A tag comes into being the first time we use it. The coffee question now wears a **#question** pill, and from here on #question sits in the list like any other tag.

![The coffee question wearing its new #question pill](/images/get-started/question-tagged.webp)

More questions will turn up as the day goes on. Later we'll gather every one that's still open into a single list, wherever we happened to write it.

## A first real task

Dana gave us some homework. Click at the end of **First week is mostly reading and meeting people**, press `Enter`, and type `Read the onboarding handbook #tas`. **#task** comes with a new vault, and there it is at the top of the list, **Something to do.** Press `Enter` to take it.

A #task gets a checkbox of its own straight away. But look up at the **TODAY'S TASKS** box. It still says **Nothing scheduled for today.** Writing a task on today's page doesn't mean we plan to do it today. We might read the handbook tomorrow, or next week. So we tell Vellum when.

In the **TODAY'S TASKS** box, click **+ Add to today**.

![The Add to today list, with Read the onboarding handbook at the top marked Unscheduled, and the sample tasks under it](/images/get-started/add-to-today.webp)

This list shows the tasks we already have. Pick one, and it goes on today. Nothing new gets made. Our handbook is at the top, marked **Unscheduled**. (Under it are the sample tasks a new vault comes with, for a garden we'll probably never plant.) Press `Enter` to add our onboarding task, then `Escape` to close the list.

![The handbook in the Today's tasks box, and down in the meeting the same task marked today, with When September 25, 2026 under it](/images/get-started/task-in-box.webp)

There it is, in the box. And down in the meeting, the handbook's line now has a small **today** at its end, with **When** and today's date underneath. It's the same task in both places, not a copy. Tick it in the box or in the meeting, and it's ticked in both.

We won't tick it just yet. It's a long handbook. And if it isn't done by tonight, it'll be waiting in the box tomorrow morning.

## Fields

Remember the faint line that turned up under the handbook, **When** and today's date? That's a **field**: a named detail on a note, with something filled in. **When** is a field for, well, *when* we want to work on the task, and we set it without even noticing, when we added the handbook to today. Fields are sometimes referred to as properties.

Tags often come with fields. #task brought **When** (and a few more fields we'll meet later on). #person brought two of its own (Role and How we met), and Dana's fields are still empty. Let's fill them in.

### Dana's details

Open Dana's page by clicking her bullet on today's page. Under **FIELDS** there's **Role** and **How we met**, both reading **None**. Click the box beside **Role**, type `Engineering manager`, and press `Enter`. Then click the box beside **How we met**, type `She ran the welcome meeting`, and press `Enter`.

Every person gets these two fields, because they belong to (are properties of) the #person tag. Marcus Aurelius has these fields, Yo-Yo Ma has them, and so will everyone we meet today.

### A field of our own

There's one more thing we'll want to know about everybody at work: which team they're on. #person has no field for that. So let's add one.

Under Dana's fields, click **+ Add field** and type `Team`.

![The Add field box with Team typed, offering New field Team](/images/get-started/team-new.webp)

The box searches every field the vault already has, in case one of them would do. There's no Team yet, so the top line offers **New field "Team"**. Press `Enter`.

A small card opens for setting the field up. Look at **Belongs to**. It says **The Fields list**, which would put Team on Dana alone. We want it on everybody, so click that box and choose **#person**, **Every note wearing it gets the field**.

![The Belongs to box open: The Fields list, #person, and Another tag](/images/get-started/team-belongs.webp)

The card says it plainly: **Team joins #person. Every note wearing the tag gets it, and it shows on the tag's own page.**

So a field can sit on a single note on its own, or belong to a tag, so that every note wearing the tag gets it. Most of the time, the tag is what we want.

Next, **Type**. It says **text**, which means we'd type the team's name by hand every time. Click it to see what else a field can be.

![The Type list: text, date, number, select, multi select, reference, image, saved search](/images/get-started/team-types.webp)

- **text** is any words. Role is text.
- **date** is a day, picked from a calendar. The handbook's When is a date.
- **number** is for things we count or add up, like a price or a page count or a rating.
- **select** is one choice from a short list we make up.
- **multi select** is the same, with more than one choice allowed.
- **reference** points at another note. We'll meet this one properly a little later.
- **image** holds a picture.
- **saved search** is one for another day.

A team is one of a handful, so pick **select**. An **Options** box appears. Type `Engineering, Design, Sales`, then click an empty part of the card, and the three turn into chips.

![The Team card set up: Type select, the three options as chips, and Belongs to #person](/images/get-started/team-card.webp)

Click **Done**. Dana now has a third field, **Team**, reading **None**. Click it and select **Engineering**.

![Dana's fields filled in: Role Engineering manager, How we met She ran the welcome meeting, Team Engineering](/images/get-started/dana-fields.webp)

### Fields go where the note goes

Press `Cmd-D` to go back to today, and look under Dana's line.

![Today's page, with Dana's three fields in a faint line under her name](/images/get-started/today-fields.webp)

Her details are here as well, in a faint line under her name: **Role** Engineering manager, **How we met** She ran the welcome meeting, **Team** Engineering. We filled them in on her page, and they show up here on today's page without us doing a thing.

### Opening a link right where it is

We could click Priya's name to go to her page and fill in her fields. But we're in the middle of the day's notes, and we don't really want to leave. So hold `Cmd` and click **Priya Shah** in the meeting line.

![Priya opened under the meeting line, in a box of her own, with her fields filled in](/images/get-started/priya-filled.webp)

Priya opens right there, under the line, in a box of her own, with her pill and her fields. This is the real Priya, not a copy. Fill her in: **Role** `Product designer`, **How we met** `My onboarding buddy`, and for **Team** pick **Design**. Then `Cmd`-click her name again to close the box.

## Today and the Library

Look at the bottom of today's page. Priya and Sam are sitting there, below the meetings, as notes of their own. Dana is tucked inside the welcome meeting. That's where we made them, and it works. But it's a little odd, isn't it? Priya isn't something that happened today. She'll still be around next week, and next month.

That's the difference between the two places a note can live in Vellum.

- **Daily Notes** is for what happens on a day: meetings, thoughts, whatever we jotted down as it came. Each day's page fills up as the day goes on, and tomorrow gets a fresh one.
- **The Library** is for things that aren't tied to a day: people, projects, a recipe, a reading list. The things that outlast the day.

Nothing breaks if a person lives on a day. A link finds its note wherever that note lives. But people outlast the day, so let's keep them in the Library.

Remember the line from this morning, **Dana Whitfield wants a few lines at five on how the day went**? It's five.

A few lines means sentences, not bullets. That's what a **document** is for: a note whose page is for writing, plain flowing prose, the way a regular ole word processor works.

Click **+ add item** at the bottom of today's page and type `5:00 pm My first day, for Dana`. Then, with the cursor still in that line, press `Cmd-K`, type `document`, and press `Enter` on **Convert to document**.

A small page icon appears in front of the line. That's how a document shows on the page. Click the icon to open it.

A document's page has its title at the top and room to write underneath. Click just under the title and start typing. A few things are handy to know as you go:

- Start a line with `## ` (two hashes and a space) for a heading.
- Start a line with `- ` (a dash and a space) for a list. `Enter` carries the list on to the next line, `Enter` twice ends it, and once more leaves a blank line.
- Put two asterisks on each side of some words, `**like this**`, to make them bold.
- `[[` works here just as it does in bullets.

Here's what we wrote. Type the same, or write your own. (Where it says `[[Priya` and `[[Sam`, pick them from the list as usual.)

```text
A good first day. The welcome meeting set out the plan for the month, and [[Priya has been a great guide.

## What I got done
- Laptop, charger and badge
- Password manager and VPN, thanks to [[Sam
- Started the onboarding handbook

## Still to find out
Who books the meeting rooms, and where the expense forms go. Both are on my list.

**Most important discovery:** the good coffee is at Grind & Co.
```

![The write-up: a paragraph linking Priya Shah, two headings, a list linking Sam Lee, and a bold line about the coffee](/images/get-started/writeup.webp)

The marks we typed step back once we move away ... the `##` fades into the margin, the dashes turn grey, and the asterisks disappear, leaving just the bold. Under the writing there's a line across the page, with **+ add item** beneath it. A document can still hold bullets of its own, below its prose, whenever that's useful. (The skillet chicken recipe that came with the vault is a document like this, with its ingredients as a checklist under the method.)

## Wrapping up

One new tag, two new fields, a new section on every person, a project, a saved search and a document. And a whole first day.

What it gets us: a page for everyone we met, which fills itself with the questions waiting for them and every place we mentioned them. A project that gathers its own tasks. A list of open questions that tidies itself as they get answered. And a page for the day, written one line at a time, that reads back like a diary.

The ideas underneath it all:

- **Everything is a note.** A line on today's page, a person, a project, a document, a search. Any note can be opened as a page of its own, and can hold notes of its own.
- **A tag says what kind of thing a note is.** It brings fields and sections with it, and it has a page of its own listing every note that wears it.
- **Links work both ways.** Link a note with `[[`, or pick it in a reference field, and the note on the other end knows about it: a count, its References, and sections that fill themselves in.
- **Days are for what happens. The Library is for what lasts.** Write things down where you are, and move them later if they outlast the day.
- **Tasks turn up where they're needed.** In the box on today's page, in the Tasks tabs, and on the project they belong to.
- **A saved search answers a question, and keeps on answering it.**

Nothing here is special to a new job. Swap the tags and the same few ideas run a house move, a class you teach, a book club or a research project. The notes point, and the pages gather.
